# JTBD interview guides and analysis

Read this when the study is about a switching decision: why someone adopted, dropped, or
considered an alternative to a product, tool, or workflow. This is a specific lens for
adoption, churn, and competitive-displacement research. It is not a default structure for
general discovery interviews.

## Interview guide structure (Moesta timeline method)

Six-stage timeline walkthrough, from *Competing Against Luck*:

- **First thought.** When the idea of a different way of doing things first occurred to them.
- **Passive looking.** When they started noticing options without actively searching.
- **Active looking.** When they began seriously evaluating alternatives.
- **Deciding.** The moment the decision got made, and what tipped it.
- **First use.** What happened when they actually started using the new thing.
- **Ongoing use.** How the relationship with the solution has evolved since.

Three to four probes per stage. Ask what was happening in their life or work at each stage, not
just about the product. A switching decision is rarely about the product in isolation. It is
usually tangled up with a change in circumstance, and the circumstance is the part that
generalizes to other buyers.

## Four forces (deep dive, after the timeline)

- **Push.** What is wrong with the current situation that is driving them to look.
- **Pull.** What is attractive about the new solution.
- **Anxiety.** What is holding them back, doubts about the new option.
- **Habit.** The pull of the status quo, comfort with the current way even where it is flawed.

Push and pull get reported far more often than anxiety and habit, because they are what people
volunteer. Anxiety and habit are usually where the stalled deals live, so probe them
deliberately.

## Desired outcomes

Ask what "better" would look like, in their own words. Resist translating this into feature
language during the interview. That translation happens in analysis, not in the room. A
participant who hears their words turned into a feature name will start answering in feature
names.

## Adapting the guide by participant type

- **Churned users.** Exit-interview framing. Focus on push and the moment the relationship
  ended.
- **Prospects who have not switched.** Hypothetical framing. Probe intention rather than lived
  experience, and flag in the writeup that this is stated intention, not observed behavior.
- **Existing users.** Full timeline walkthrough, all four forces.

## Analysis output

- Core job statement, in the format: "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can
  [outcome]."
- Push, pull, anxiety, and habit forces mapped with supporting quotes, ranked by how strongly
  each showed up across participants.
- Desired outcomes, with the evidence for each.
- Competing solutions the participant considered or was actively using, meaning what they were
  firing in favor of the new one. This includes non-products: a spreadsheet, a colleague, doing
  nothing.
- Unmet jobs, meaning outcomes nobody currently serves well. These are the product
  opportunities and the reason to run this method rather than general discovery.

## When to use this instead of general thematic analysis

General synthesis answers "what did we learn." This answers "why did they switch." Use it
specifically for adoption, churn, and competitive-displacement research.
