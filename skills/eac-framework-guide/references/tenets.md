# The tenets: full text

## How the framework is structured

A working guide for product managers in **Engagement Orchestration**. Three uses, stated on
the framework's own "How to use" page: a reference for onboarding new PMs, a calibration tool
for existing PMs, and a shared vocabulary for managers and their teams.

Three structural pieces:

| Piece | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Foundational tier** | Personal behaviors and AI judgement. The always-on capabilities every PM grows continuously. |
| **Lifecycle stages** | Ideation, Analyze, Plan, Develop, Launch, Operate. AI accelerates each stage; the PM owns the decisions. |
| **Card structure** | Every card pairs "capabilities you'll develop" with "how you'll show your manager." Calibrate together, quarterly. |

**What's changed in this revision (framework's own words):** AI is now the default working
tool, not a sidetrack. Every stage card assumes AI does the first pass; the PM directs,
challenges, and decides. A new foundational section, AI judgement, names the cognitive skills
this requires.

## Coverage: what this skill has and does not have

⚠️ **This skill holds the Personal behaviors cards only.** Be honest about that in every
answer. Do not imply the eight cards below are the whole framework.

**Present (8 cards, all Personal behaviors or previews):** Coalition building, Know the who,
Know the why, Batteries included, Emotional intelligence, Driving to resolution, Executive
presence, Build a winning team.

**Absent, and known to exist:**
- The **AI judgement** foundational section. Explicitly called out as new in this revision, so
  it is likely the most current and most emphasised part of the framework.
- All six **Lifecycle stage** cards: Ideation, Analyze, Plan, Develop, Launch, Operate.

If a question touches AI judgement or any lifecycle stage, say the card is not loaded here and
point the user at the deck. Do not improvise a card that exists and that you have not read.
Answering from the Personal behaviors cards alone, when the real answer sits in a stage card,
is the main way this skill can mislead.

## Reading the cards below

Capabilities and evidence rows are **not** 1:1. Most cards have more capabilities than evidence
rows, because one piece of evidence can carry several capabilities.

Every card's calibration partner is **your manager (Product Director)**. The suffix below says
*where* the behavior is observed, which is the useful part.

Tier legend:
- **Foundation · Personal behaviors:** expected at current level, calibrated now.
- **Preview:** a look ahead at Director scope. *Not* a current-level bar. See
  `playbooks.md` § Preview cards.
- **Tier unconfirmed:** the source card's header was not captured. Treat as current-level
  unless it is one of the two previews, which state their preview status in their own purpose
  line.

**Card numbering below is this skill's own, for reference only. The source cards are not
numbered and the canonical order is unknown.**

## 1. Coalition building
**Tier:** Foundation · Personal behaviors
**Purpose:** Build alliances across NiCE and broker between human and AI-augmented teams.
**Observed:** across R&D, Architect, UX, GTM partnerships

**Capabilities**
1. **Meeting design** — fewer, sharper, decision-driven
2. **Cross-team trust** — build relationships of trust, responsiveness and knowledge across adjacent product, engineering, and GTM teams
3. **Win-win outcomes** — negotiate outcomes when stakeholder priorities collide
4. **Signal reconciliation** — reconcile competing data or signal sources across teams to find shared ground
5. **Orchestration over ownership** — lead 'what can we do together' across teams
6. **Suite-connection impact** — connect your workstream to adjacent Suite priorities, so impact compounds beyond your own area

**Evidence**
1. Walks manager through partnerships built and how those have added value
2. Uses examples to show effective meeting design and win-win negotiation outcomes
3. Names a decision where they aligned a cross-team disagreement
4. Walks manager through a decision where they shaped their own roadmap around an adjacent workstream's priority, and the impact that connection created

## 2. Know the who
**Tier:** Foundation · Personal behaviors
**Purpose:** Map the terrain and know your way around it. Identify who owns what and understand who you serve.
**Observed:** behavior calibrated quarterly

**Capabilities**
1. **Ownership tracing** — trace who owns, decides, or advises on an activity, even when it is undocumented
2. **Mandate literacy** — understand *why* a role owns what it owns, not just that it does, well enough to anticipate how they will respond
3. **Contact center fluency** — understand the operational pressures and stakes of the contact center well enough to catch when a solution ignores them
4. **Persona range** — engage the distinct pressures and priorities across contact center personas rather than treating the vertical as one audience

**Evidence**
1. Walks manager through a moment where they traced full ownership for an undocumented activity and engaged the right person directly
2. Walks manager through a moment where understanding a role's underlying mandate, not just their ownership, changed how they approached a conversation or ask
3. Walks manager through where they consulted the persona asset or Teams agent to inform a product decision, what changes as a result, and what they would have done without that context
4. Walks manager through how they adapted a decision or pitch for two distinct contact center personas whose priorities pulled in different directions

---

## 3. Know the why
**Tier:** Foundation · Personal behaviors
**Purpose:** Paint the picture and sell the vision. Articulate why we invest, the value to customers, and the value to NiCE.
**Observed:** behavior calibrated quarterly

**Capabilities**
1. **Big-picture evangelism** - connect technology choices to customer outcomes rather than features
2. **Strategic framing** - defend the why under executive pressure, with the argument intact
3. **Targeted communication** - deliver the why to many audiences and personas, adjusting the message and the proof for each
4. **Business case craft** - articulate value, cost, and risk in one breath
5. **AI fluency** - understand what AI can and cannot do, and use that judgement to justify fit for the problem at hand

**Evidence**
1. Walks manager through how they framed the same why differently for development, peers, WLT, and SMEs, and why each version was the right call for that audience
2. Walks manager through a moment in the prioritization process where the why met resistance, and what changed the room's mind
3. Walks manager through why AI was the right fit for their last feature investment, including what alternative they ruled out and why AI won

> `AI fluency` here is about justifying *fit* for a problem. It is adjacent to the separate
> **AI judgement** foundational section, which this skill does not hold. If a question is really
> about AI judgement as a capability set, say so rather than stretching this one bullet to cover it.

## 4. Batteries included
**Tier:** Foundation · Personal behaviors
**Purpose:** Self-motivation, action bias, and creativity in an AI-amplified environment.
**Observed:** in delivery and adoption choices

**Capabilities**
1. **Action bias** — ship, learn, iterate; AI compresses the cost of trying
2. **Creativity** — the human edge AI cannot copy
3. **Fearless decision-making** — make the call with imperfect information
4. **Adaptability** — your tools will change again next quarter
5. **Tool curiosity** — explore new tooling without chasing every shiny thing
6. **Retrospective learning** — revisit past decisions to capture what worked and what didn't, and apply it to the next call

**Evidence**
1. Level-sets with manager on current performance across these behaviors
2. Provides examples of decisions made with incomplete data and how they adapted
3. Names a tool they tried, kept, or discarded, and explains the why
4. Walks manager through a decision they revisited, what they learned, and how it changed a subsequent call

## 5. Emotional intelligence
**Tier:** Foundation · Personal behaviors
**Purpose:** EQ matters more, not less, when AI handles analytical heavy lifting.
**Observed:** in stakeholder interactions

**Capabilities**
1. **Active listening** — hear customers, engineers, and GTM, including the silent failures of AI and the perspectives that oppose your own
2. **Positive mental attitude** — durable under uncertainty
3. **Honest and authentic** — including with yourself about what you do not know
4. **Passionate advocate** — for the customer, not for the technology
5. **Read the room** — notice what AI cannot, and have the courage to say the thing

**Evidence**
1. Level-sets with manager on current performance across EQ behaviors
2. Builds a plan to improve specific areas and tracks progress over time
3. Names a moment where human judgement clearly outperformed AI synthesis
4. Names a decision where they sought out a dissenting or opposing view before deciding, and how it changed or confirmed the call

## 6. Driving to resolution
**Tier:** Tier unconfirmed (header not captured). Treat as current-level.
**Purpose:** Take accountability for outcomes beyond your own scope, and see cross-Suite ambiguity through to a decision.
**Observed:** in cross-Suite escalations and stalled threads

**Capabilities**
1. **Claim the ambiguity** — step into an unowned issue to drive it to a decision or a named owner, without assuming you now own it forever
2. **Sustainable resolution** — redirect recurring requests to a resource that lets the requestor self-serve going forward
3. **Close unowned threads** — end a stalled cross-Suite thread with a decision, a named owner, or an explicit "not resolving"
4. **Escalate on purpose** — push a stalled cross-Suite decision upward before it dies quietly

**Evidence**
1. Names an unowned issue they picked up, and how they got it to a decision or an owner without absorbing it permanently
2. Walks manager through a recurring request they redirected to a self-service resource, and evidence the request stopped recurring
3. Names a cross-Suite thread they closed that had been open for weeks, and what closing it actually meant — decision, owner, or explicit non-resolution
4. Names a moment they escalated a stalled cross-Suite decision rather than letting it drift, and what happened as a result

## 7. Executive presence
**Tier:** Preview — Director-level. Not a current-level bar.
**Purpose:** A preview of Director-level executive engagement. These capabilities come into focus as you prepare for promotion, and as opportunities with executives, customers, and analysts arise.
**Observed:** across exec, customer, and analyst interactions

**Capabilities**
1. **Company representation** — represent the company's philosophy, values, and confidentiality
2. **Audience management** — know and manage your audience — executive, customer, analyst — and recognize how credibility with one audience opens doors to broader settings, like NiCE World
3. **Ownership and accountability** — including for AI features in production
4. **De-escalation** — detoxify the environment when stakes rise
5. **Speak to AI bets** — with confidence, calibration, and humility

**Evidence**
1. Level-sets with manager on current performance against the executive presence rubric
2. Plans improvement actions and tracks them over multiple quarters
3. Shows examples of executive, customer, or analyst engagement, or, where the access has not yet come, how they have actively sought it out

> Note the escape hatch in evidence #3: access to executives is not always available to a
> PM. "How you have actively sought it out" counts. Do not treat absent exec exposure as a
> gap in the person.

## 8. Build a winning team
**Tier:** Preview — Product Director responsibilities. Not a current-level bar.
**Purpose:** A preview of Product Director responsibilities. These capabilities become relevant as you prepare for promotion, so you can start building them ahead of time.
**Observed:** with your manager, and HR partners on recruiting and development

**Capabilities**
1. **Recruiting judgement** — identify and attract the right talent, including AI fluency, without over-indexing on it
2. **Coaching for judgement** — coach the thinking behind decisions, not just tool usage
3. **Reward and retain** — recognize human work that AI cannot do
4. **Succession planning** — freely empower team members to eventually grow into your role, or your supervisor's
5. **R&D partnership** — build the relationships with R&D that let a team execute well together
6. **AI-aware team culture** — build a culture where overriding AI is rewarded when it is right

**Evidence**
1. Level-sets with manager on current understanding of these responsibilities
2. Researches new approaches to recruiting and team building, and shares findings
3. Articulates an early succession or growth plan for people around them, even without formal authority yet

## Card footer (all non-preview cards)

Each card carries two open fields the PM and manager fill in together:

- **Calibration:** add manager calibration
- **Notes & feedback:** capture between PM and manager

Status chip on each card: `Not started` → (in progress / calibrated as the manager updates it).
