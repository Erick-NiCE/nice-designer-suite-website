# Playbooks

**Scope reminder:** this skill holds the Personal behaviors cards only. The AI judgement
foundational section and the six lifecycle-stage cards (Ideation, Analyze, Plan, Develop,
Launch, Operate) are not here. Name that gap rather than answering around it.

Procedures for the four things people actually arrive wanting. Read the one that matches;
don't run all four.

---

## § Preview cards: read this first, always

**Executive presence** and **Build a winning team** are labelled previews of Director /
Product Director scope. They exist so a PM can start building ahead of promotion. They are
**not** current-level expectations.

Consequences for every answer you give:

- Never report them as gaps, misses, or fails. A PM with nothing to show on "Build a winning
  team" is not behind.
- In a self-assessment, cover the six non-preview cards. Handle the two previews
  separately, framed as *"where you could start"*, not *"where you're short"*.
- If the user asks for an overall readout, give it on the six, then add the previews as a
  forward-looking section.
- The one exception: if the user says they are actively preparing a promotion case, treat the
  previews as in scope: that is exactly what they are for. Ask before assuming.

Do not soften the other cards the same way. Those six *are* the current bar.

## 1. Explain / look up

**"What are the tenets?" · "What's the coalition building one?" · "Which tenets cover X?"**

- Whole-framework question → name all eight with their purpose lines, grouped by tier,
  **and say that the AI judgement section and the six lifecycle-stage cards are not loaded here**
  (Personal behaviors ×6, Preview ×2). Don't dump every capability; that's ~40 bullets and
  nobody reads it.
- Single card → the purpose line, the capabilities, and where it's observed. Add the evidence
  column only if they ask what to show, or the question is clearly about demonstrating it.
- Fuzzy match on card names. "Know the who" is easy to mangle ("know your who", "know the
  room"); "Batteries included" gets called "action bias" or "self-motivation" after its lead
  capability. Match on capability names too, not just card titles.

**Routing a situation to a card** is the most common real question, and the cards overlap.
Disambiguate on the *mechanism*, not the vibe:

| Situation | Card | Why not the neighbour |
|---|---|---|
| Two teams want incompatible things from your roadmap | Coalition building | It's a negotiation between owners, not an unowned gap |
| Nobody owns this and it's rotting | Driving to resolution | The problem is absence of an owner, not disagreement between owners |
| You can't work out who decides this | Know the who | Tracing, not resolving. Resolution comes after |
| You shipped with half the data | Batteries included | Fearless decision-making, #3 |
| You knew the room was unhappy and said nothing | Emotional intelligence | "Read the room", the courage half, #5 |
| A thread has been open six weeks | Driving to resolution | #3, close unowned threads |
| An exec pushed back on why you are investing here | Know the why | #2, strategic framing. Defending the why, not tracing owners |
| You pitched the same thing differently to R&D and to WLT | Know the why | #3, targeted communication |
| You had to justify choosing AI for a feature | Know the why | #5 / evidence #3. Not Batteries included, which is about tool adoption for yourself |
| Same request lands on you every month | Driving to resolution | #2, sustainable resolution. The answer is a self-serve resource, not answering faster |

## 2. Build the evidence for a calibration conversation

**"Help me prep for my 1:1" · "What do I show for coalition building?" · "I did X: where does it count?"**

This is what the right-hand column of each card is for. Work backwards from it.

1. **Pick the card(s).** If they described work, route it with the table above. One piece of
   work often lands on two cards: say so, and say which is the stronger claim.
2. **Read the evidence rows verbatim.** They are specific about form. Notice the recurring
   verbs: *names* a moment, *walks manager through* a decision, *shows* examples. Every row
   wants a **single concrete instance**, not a capability claim. "I'm good at cross-team
   trust" satisfies nothing; "R&D shipped the schema change two sprints early because I'd
   already built the relationship with their architect" satisfies Coalition building #1.
3. **Interrogate the story against the row's real requirement.** Most rows have a second
   clause people skip:
   - Coalition building #4 wants the *impact the connection created*, not just that you
     aligned with an adjacent workstream.
   - Know the who #3 wants **what you would have done without that context**: the
     counterfactual is the point. No counterfactual, no evidence.
   - Know the who #2 wants the *mandate*, not the org chart: why they own it, and how
     knowing that changed your approach.
   - Know the why #1 wants *why each version was the right call for that audience*, not just
     that you tailored the message.
   - Know the why #2 wants **what changed the room's mind**. Resistance you outlasted is not
     resistance you answered.
   - Know the why #3 wants **the alternative you ruled out and why AI won**. An AI feature with
     no discarded alternative does not satisfy this row.
   - Batteries included #3 wants the **why** on keeping or discarding a tool. "I tried it"
     is not the answer.
   - Batteries included #4 and Emotional intelligence #4 both want a **changed or confirmed
     call**: the loop has to close.
   - Driving to resolution #1 wants "without absorbing it permanently." If they still own it,
     that's a partial.
   - Driving to resolution #2 wants **evidence the request stopped recurring**. A published doc
     with no drop in inbound is not this row.
   - Driving to resolution #3 wants what closing it *actually meant*: decision, owner, or an
     explicit "not resolving." All three count. Explicit non-resolution is a legitimate close.
4. **Name what's thin.** Tell them which rows they cannot currently fill, and what the smallest
   real piece of work is that would fill one. Not a suggestion to "get more exposure": a
   specific thread to go close this month.
5. **Write it in their voice, short.** A calibration note is a few sentences per row that a
   manager can read in a 1:1, not a self-review essay.

## 3. Self-assessment / gap analysis

**"How am I doing against these?" · "Where should I focus this quarter?"**

Do not invent a numeric score; the framework has status chips (`Not started` → in progress →
calibrated) and a manager calibration field, not a rating scale. Mirror that.

1. Five cards in scope (four Foundation + Driving to resolution). Previews handled per § above.
2. Per card, sort the evidence rows into: **can name an instance today** / **has something
   partial** / **nothing yet**. Ask the user for their instances; do not infer them from
   context you don't have.
3. Look for the cross-card pattern rather than reporting five independent scores. The common
   shapes:
   - Strong on Coalition building, thin on Driving to resolution → good at getting agreement
     between owners, avoids the unowned stuff.
   - Strong on Know the who, thin on Coalition building → maps the terrain, doesn't convert
     the map into alliances.
   - Strong on Batteries included, thin on Emotional intelligence → ships fast, misses the room.
   - Strong on Know the who, thin on Know the why → knows the terrain and the owners, cannot
     yet make anyone care.
   - Strong on Know the why, thin on Know the who → sells the vision into the wrong room.
   - Thin on Driving to resolution #2 specifically, strong elsewhere on that card → resolves
     things heroically and repeatedly instead of structurally.
4. Recommend **one** card to work on next quarter, with the specific evidence row to target
   and one piece of real work that would produce it. One, not a development plan with five
   workstreams.
5. Say plainly that this is self-assessment. Calibration is the manager's, quarterly, and it
   may not agree.

## 4. Manager-side: calibrating someone else

**"Help me calibrate my PM" · "Write feedback on X against the tenets"**

- Calibrate against **observed behavior in the stated venue**, which each card names: R&D /
  Architect / UX / GTM partnerships; stakeholder interactions; delivery and adoption choices;
  cross-Suite escalations and stalled threads. If you haven't observed them in that venue,
  say so instead of scoring it.
- Feedback goes on the *mechanism*, not the person. "The thread on X sat for five weeks and
  closed only after Y escalated" is calibratable; "needs more drive" is not.
- Every card's evidence column is a **conversation prompt**, not a checklist to mark. The
  design intent is that the PM walks the manager through it. Preserve that: draft the
  question you'd ask, not the verdict you'd deliver.
- Cover the strong cards too. A calibration that only names gaps is not a calibration.
