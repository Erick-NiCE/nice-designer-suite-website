# EAC Framework Guide

The EAC Team's behavior framework, which NiCE product managers are developed and calibrated
against. Eight cards, each with a purpose, the capabilities you build, and the part that makes
it usable: **an evidence column stating exactly what you show your manager.**

The framework is a **development instrument, not a rating scale.** Every card's calibration
partner is your manager (Product Director), calibrated quarterly, with an open calibration
field and a shared notes field the PM and manager fill in together. There is no score. Hold
that line: give readouts in the framework's own terms (`Not started` → in progress →
calibrated, and per-row "can you name an instance?"), never a number you invented.

## What the framework is

A working guide for product managers in **Engagement Orchestration**, used three ways: a
reference for onboarding new PMs, a calibration tool for existing PMs, and a shared vocabulary
between managers and their teams.

It has three structural pieces:

- **Foundational tier:** Personal behaviors *and* AI judgement. Always-on, grown continuously.
- **Lifecycle stages:** Ideation, Analyze, Plan, Develop, Launch, Operate. AI accelerates each
  stage; the PM owns the decisions.
- **Card structure:** every card pairs capabilities with "how you'll show your manager,"
  calibrated together, quarterly.

The framework's own stated shift: AI is the default working tool, not a sidetrack. Every stage
card assumes AI does the first pass and the PM directs, challenges, and decides.

## ⚠️ Coverage: Personal behaviors only

**This skill holds the Personal behaviors cards. It does not hold the AI judgement
foundational section, and it does not hold any of the six Lifecycle stage cards.** Say so when
it matters. Never improvise a card you have not read, and never let an answer imply the eight
cards below are the whole framework. If the question is really about AI judgement or a
lifecycle stage, name the gap and point the user at the deck.

## The eight cards

**Foundation · Personal behaviors.** The current-level bar, calibrated now.
1. **Coalition building:** build alliances across NiCE and broker between human and AI-augmented teams.
2. **Know the who:** map the terrain. Who owns what, and who you serve.
3. **Know the why:** paint the picture and sell the vision. Why we invest, value to customers, value to NiCE.
4. **Batteries included:** self-motivation, action bias, and creativity in an AI-amplified environment.
5. **Emotional intelligence:** EQ matters more, not less, when AI handles analytical heavy lifting.
6. **Driving to resolution:** see cross-Suite ambiguity through to a decision. (Tier unconfirmed; treat as current-level.)

**Preview.** A look ahead at Director scope. **Not a current-level bar.**
7. **Executive presence:** Director-level executive, customer, and analyst engagement.
8. **Build a winning team:** Product Director responsibilities, previewed early.

Numbering is this skill's own. The source cards are not numbered and the canonical order is
unknown.

⚠️ **The preview distinction is the easiest way to get this wrong.** Cards 7 and 8 say so on
their face: they exist so a PM can start building *ahead of promotion*. Never report them as
gaps or misses. See `references/playbooks.md` § Preview cards before any assessment.

## The four things this does

**1. Explain and route.** Name a card, or map a situation onto one. Routing is the more common
ask and the cards overlap: disambiguate on mechanism, not vibe. *Unowned and rotting* is
Driving to resolution; *two owners who disagree* is Coalition building; *can't tell who
decides* is Know the who. Full table in `playbooks.md`.

**2. Prep evidence.** The highest-value use. Each card's right-hand column specifies the form:
*names* a moment, *walks manager through* a decision, *shows* examples. Every row wants **one
concrete instance**, and most rows have a second clause people skip: the counterfactual in Know the who #3, the *impact* in Coalition building #4, "evidence the request stopped
recurring" in Driving to resolution #2. Interrogate the story against the full row, not the
headline. Then say which rows they still can't fill and name the one piece of work that would
fill one.

**3. Self-assess.** Sort evidence rows into can-name / partial / nothing-yet across the five
in-scope cards, then find the **cross-card pattern** rather than reporting five verdicts
(strong Coalition building + thin Driving to resolution = good at agreement between owners,
avoids the unowned stuff). Recommend **one** card for next quarter, with a specific target row.
Ask the user for their instances: never infer someone's evidence from context you don't have.

**4. Calibrate someone else.** Each card names the venue the behavior is observed in; if the
manager hasn't observed them there, say so instead of scoring it. Feedback on the mechanism,
not the person. And draft the *question to ask*: the evidence column is a conversation prompt
by design, where the PM walks the manager through it, not a checklist a manager marks.

## Rules

- **Never fabricate someone's evidence or status.** If you don't have the instance, ask for it.
  Every card is calibrated against observed behavior; inventing an observation is the one
  failure mode that makes this skill worse than nothing.
- **Cards 7 and 8 are not a bar.** Repeat as needed.
- **No invented scores, ratings, or percentages.** The framework doesn't have them.
- **Quote evidence rows verbatim when precision matters**: the second clauses carry the
  requirement, and paraphrasing drops them.
- **Never answer a lifecycle-stage or AI-judgement question from these cards.** Name the gap.
- **This is a development framework, not a performance verdict.** Manager calibration is the
  manager's, quarterly, and a self-assessment may not agree with it. Say so when it's relevant.
