# The 34 themes, read for design work

Hand-authored — **not** generated by `npm run gen:strengths`, unlike `roster.md` and
`theme-index.md`. The definitions here are condensed in our own words; the canonical Gallup
wording lives on the grid site's `glossary.html`, and that's what to point a person at if they
want the official definition of their own theme.

Each theme gets three things:

- **Is** — what the theme actually is, in one line.
- **On a design team** — where it shows up in our work: research, critique, systems, delivery.
- **Overplayed** — the failure mode when it runs unchecked. This is the half that makes coaching
  useful, and the half people skip. Never present it as a flaw; it's the same engine in the wrong gear.

Domain membership is fixed: 8 Strategic Thinking, 8 Influencing, 9 Relationship Building,
9 Executing.

---

## Strategic Thinking — takes in information and figures out what it means

| Theme | Is | On a design team | Overplayed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Analytical** | Wants the reason behind the claim. | The person who asks what the sample size was, or whether that lift is real. Pressure-tests a design rationale before a VP does. | Stalls a decision waiting for evidence that was never going to exist. Can read as sceptical of a colleague rather than of the data. |
| **Context** | Understands the present by studying how it got here. | Knows why the pattern is the way it is, which stops the team re-litigating a decision made three years ago. Valuable in design-system archaeology. | Anchors on precedent. "We tried that" becomes a reason not to try it again under different conditions. |
| **Futuristic** | Energised by what could be. | Draws the two-years-out version that makes the next-quarter version make sense. Good at getting a room to want something. | Sells a vision the team has no path to. Loses interest once the idea needs to survive a sprint. |
| **Ideation** | Fascinated by ideas and the connections between them. | Divergence. Fills the wall in a workshop, finds the framing nobody else saw. | Keeps opening options after the team needed to close. Treats convergence as a loss. |
| **Input** | Collects and keeps things that might be useful. | The team's memory — has the competitor teardown, the old research deck, the reference that unblocks you. | Gathers instead of deciding. Hands over 40 links when the ask was a recommendation. |
| **Intellection** | Thinks by thinking; needs quiet processing time. | Depth. Catches the structural problem in a flow that everyone else skimmed past. | Goes quiet in fast rooms and is read as disengaged. Needs the agenda in advance to do their best work — and often doesn't ask for it. |
| **Learner** | Energised by the process of getting better, not just the result. | Picks up a new tool, domain, or research method fast. Cheapest path to filling a skills gap. | Chases novelty past the point of usefulness. Starts more than finishes. |
| **Strategic** | Sees the paths through, fast. | Given a mess, comes back with three routes and the trade-off between them. Very high leverage in planning. | Skips the explanation because the answer was obvious to them, leaving the team unconvinced and uncommitted. |

## Influencing — takes charge, speaks up, gets an idea heard

| Theme | Is | On a design team | Overplayed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Activator** | Starts. Impatient for motion. | Breaks analysis paralysis. Ships the rough version that teaches the team more than another week of debate. | Moves before alignment, then the work gets redone. Reads as dismissive of people who need to think first. |
| **Command** | Comfortable taking control and saying the hard thing. | Will tell a stakeholder no. Invaluable in a room where design keeps getting overruled. | Steamrolls quieter voices; the decision is right and the team hasn't bought it. Scarce on our team (5 people), so it lands hard when it appears. |
| **Communication** | Puts things into words easily. | Turns a messy design rationale into a story a VP nods along to. Presents, narrates, writes the doc. | Persuasive ahead of being correct. Polish can carry a weak argument further than it deserves. |
| **Competition** | Measures against others. | Raises the bar by benchmarking. Wants our work to beat the competitor's, visibly. | Turns collaboration into scorekeeping. Deflated by a loss rather than informed by it. |
| **Maximizer** | Takes strong to superb. | The polish pass. Knows the difference between done and good, and cares about it. | Won't ship. Spends the last 20% of time on the last 2% of quality; disengages from work that can only ever be adequate. |
| **Self-Assurance** | Has an internal compass and trusts it. | Holds a position under pressure without needing consensus to feel safe. Steadies a wobbling project. | Doesn't update on new evidence. Confidence reads as certainty, which shuts down input. |
| **Significance** | Wants the work to matter, and to be seen mattering. | Aims at problems worth solving; won't quietly accept invisible busywork. | Optimises for visible work over necessary work. Credit becomes a live issue. |
| **Woo** | Enjoys winning new people over. | Opens doors — recruits participants, builds the cross-org relationships that get design invited earlier. | Breadth over depth; strong at the first meeting, thinner on the fifth. |

## Relationship Building — holds a team together

| Theme | Is | On a design team | Overplayed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adaptability** | Lives in the present; takes it as it comes. | Unbothered by the reprioritisation that derails everyone else. Good in ambiguity and in incident-shaped work. | No forward plan. Reactive by default, which reads as unreliable to people who need dates. |
| **Connectedness** | Believes things link up and mean something. | Sees the system behind the screens, and the human consequence behind the metric. Good ethical instinct. | Argues from meaning where the room needs evidence. |
| **Developer** | Sees potential and cultivates it. | Grows juniors. Notices the small improvement and names it, which is how people stay. | Invests in someone past the point of return. Avoids the honest hard conversation because it feels like giving up. |
| **Empathy** | Feels what others feel. | Reads the room, and reads a research participant. Catches the discomfort a usability score doesn't show. | Absorbs the team's stress. Softens necessary criticism until the message is gone. |
| **Harmony** | Prefers agreement to conflict. | Finds the practical common ground and defuses a spiralling critique. | Avoids the disagreement the design actually needed. Real objections go unsaid and resurface late. |
| **Includer** | Notices who's left out and pulls them in. | Makes sure the offshore teammate, the quiet researcher, or the neglected edge-case user is in the room. Matters a lot on a team split across three regions. | Widens the circle until decisions can't be made. Everyone gets a say on everything. |
| **Individualization** | Intrigued by what makes each person different. | Casts a project well — knows who thrives on ambiguity and who needs the brief. Our most common relationship theme (23 people). | Bespokes everything; consistency and fairness suffer. Resists a standard process that would have been fine. |
| **Positivity** | Contagious enthusiasm. | Sustains morale through a long or thankless project. Makes a workshop worth attending. | Optimism reads as not taking the problem seriously. Bad news gets softened past the point of accuracy. |
| **Relator** | Deep relationships with a few. | Builds the trust that lets a critique be blunt without being wounding. Strong long-term partnerships with engineers and PMs. | Slow to open up to new people; can look like an in-group to anyone outside it. |

## Executing — makes it actually happen

| Theme | Is | On a design team | Overplayed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Achiever** | Needs to have produced something today. | Reliable output, sustained. The person whose work is simply always there. | Measures worth by volume; burns out. Confuses busy with effective. |
| **Arranger** | Configures people and resources into a working whole. | Our most common theme by far (29 people). Juggles the multi-workstream project and reconfigures cheerfully when it changes. | Keeps reorganising past the point of value. Complexity gets managed rather than removed. |
| **Belief** | Has unchanging core values that drive purpose. | Anchors the team on user good over the convenient shortcut. Won't ship the dark pattern. | Immovable where the situation genuinely calls for pragmatism. Turns a trade-off into a moral question. |
| **Consistency** | Treats people the same; wants clear rules. | Native design-systems thinking — the one who cares that the same case is handled the same way everywhere. Scarce (5 people). | Rules over judgement. Fights a justified exception on principle. |
| **Deliberative** | Serious care in deciding; anticipates obstacles. | Spots the risk before launch. Excellent pre-mortem partner. Scarce (3 people) — a real gap for a team that ships. | Slow, and reads as pessimistic. Raises the risk without proposing the mitigation. |
| **Discipline** | Wants routine, structure, order. | Makes the recurring thing actually recur — audits, QA passes, file hygiene. Scarce (5 people). | Rigid when the plan should change. Distressed by the churn the rest of the team shrugs off. |
| **Focus** | Picks a direction and holds it. | Finishes. Says no to the interesting tangent. Our scarcest Executing theme (3 people), which is worth sitting with. | Misses the change of context that should have redirected them. Can look uncooperative when protecting the goal. |
| **Responsibility** | Psychological ownership of what they said they'd do. | It gets done, without being chased. Load-bearing for a distributed team (24 people have it). | Says yes past capacity, then absorbs the cost silently. Takes over-personal ownership of team failures. |
| **Restorative** | Good at figuring out what's broken and fixing it. | Debt paydown, a11y cleanup, the messy legacy flow nobody wants. Finds satisfaction where others find drudgery. | Drawn to problems over opportunities. Fixes what could have been deleted. |

## Reading the domain balance

`roster.md` tags each person `ST# INF# REL# EXC#` — how many of their top 10 sit in each domain.
Team baseline: **ST 30% · REL 28% · EXC 27% · INF 15%**.

Influencing is the team's structurally thin domain. Two consequences worth naming when they come up:

- A group with **INF 0–1 across everyone** will do good work and struggle to get it adopted. The gap
  isn't skill, it's advocacy — that group needs a sponsor, or needs to borrow someone with
  Communication/Command/Self-Assurance for the stakeholder moment.
- Because Influencing is scarce team-wide, the few people who carry it get pulled into every
  high-stakes room. That's a load, not just a strength.

The mirror case: a group heavy in **ST with little EXC** will generate options and not close them.
Heavy **EXC with little ST** will execute the wrong thing efficiently. Heavy **REL with little
Command/Self-Assurance** will be a pleasure to work in and slow to make an unpopular call.
